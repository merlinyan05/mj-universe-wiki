# Mistakenly commenting on a parent's account and the return of a phrase

- Type: a mechanism summary drawn from campus-exclusive video examples; it can migrate to other close-friend or family scenes, but the relationship must be stated separately.
- Characters: [Thanos](../../../characters/灭霸.md), [Raccoon](../../../characters/小浣熊.md), [Mie Jianguo](../../../characters/灭建国.md).
- Basis: [R51](../../../sources/R51.md), with transcript-assisted reading and spot checks of key frames; this is not a fixed template counted across the whole internet.

## How it unfolds

Misidentifying the account owner → leaving a mocking comment → companions cannot find the matching post, exposing the mistake → the child explains the insult away as campus slang to end the scene → the parent actually learns it → uses it again during a parent-teacher call, so the earlier explanation produces new consequences.

## Relationships and where the joke sits

Rocket holds the content first but misattributes it; Thanos gets a chance to explain and to shift the risk rather than only taking the scolding. The father is both the person wrongly criticised and the actor who walks into the teacher's call carrying the new phrase.

The information gap moves twice: at "who posted it" and at "what explanation did each person believe". A callback phrase must be traceable to the earlier learning process; the father cannot simply know what the classmates said privately for no reason.

## Difference from other patterns

Not being able to find the content here is the wrong account, not blocking. The technically obtained post in [Late-night emo, mocked the next day](深夜emo次日被嘲笑.md) is a bridge belonging to another video example and cannot be moved in to fill in the plot here. When used across scenes, the specific speaker and the misidentified account are still recorded per work.
