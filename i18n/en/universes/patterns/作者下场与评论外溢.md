# Creators joining in and comments spilling over

- Type: comment play and in-video metanarrative shared across universes; the two kinds of evidence below are labelled separately, and comments are not treated as an in-world storyline.
- Basis: [R18](../../sources/R18.md), [R21](../../sources/R21.md), [R22](../../sources/R22.md), [R24](../../sources/R24.md).

## How it unfolds

The video establishes a name pattern / aggrieved reaction → viewers take the creator's or a commenter's nickname and continue the same pattern → the creator or the original poster answers back in character → repliers keep playing the teasing friend or the parent.

After being teased with a nickname, the creator of R18 replies "I'm not playing with you any more" and writes that they run away crying; the R21 creator also answers the name with the other side's nickname. In R22 the two sides in the replies keep asking each other's names. These are on-the-spot imitations of the video's relationships; the "relatives" derived from nicknames should not be inserted into the characters' parents table.

## Another variant: viewers playing the parent

A comment in R24 questions in a father's tone who is bullying his son at school, and replies continue with the grandparents' names, even reading "Jianguo" as something he has done. The sources only support role-play and variants in the comments, and do not prove a real family, occupation or personal history.

## How to record it

Note the trigger pattern, how the reply changes tack and whether the creator takes part; summarise long third-party dialogues instead of copying them whole. State clearly "comment co-creation" or "creator's reply"; a creator taking part in the comments does not automatically make it in-video canon.

## In-video metanarrative: protesting only adds relatives

[R49](../../sources/R49.md) has the family members face netizens' protests one after another; each time they give their own name and repeat the earlier list of relatives, even the pet joins in, and the attempt to stop the spread only enlarges the family tree. This is a fictional character speaking inside the video, not a statement by a real rights holder.

## Comment proposals and claiming to be the class next door

In [R70](../../sources/R70.md) the rules-horror comments extend the snack-immunity rule, and someone else answers as a character from the class next door. Label them "viewer proposal" and "comment continuation" respectively; they cannot directly rewrite the world's rules or register an in-video cameo.

Comments in [R47](../../sources/R47.md) wish the relatives' names could be unified; this can be treated as reader feedback calling for a version comparison table, but it is not grounds for deleting other creators' explicit versions.

## Viewers playing a profession, creators answering back

Under the 11 September short video of [R72](../../sources/R72.md), a viewer describes themselves as someone who hunts this kind of fictional creature, and the creator replies that they failed at their job. The two sides in the comments co-perform an identity scene, but it does not become a hunt that already happened in the original video, and it cannot on its own give a character a death ending.
