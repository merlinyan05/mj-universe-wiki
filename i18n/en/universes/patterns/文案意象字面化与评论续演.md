# Literally acted-out copywriting imagery and comment continuation

- Type: shared across universes; the campus version most easily leads into "see you at school tomorrow".
- Basis: [R15](../../sources/R15.md), [R17](../../sources/R17.md), see also [R14](../../sources/R14.md). This round mainly checks comments and replies and does not pretend to be a complete in-video sequence.

## How it unfolds

Sincere expression of feeling → a teasing friend picks out one concrete noun → the metaphor is explained literally → the person is given a nickname instead → a third person keeps the line going or announces that they will meet the next day → the person defends their dignity and threatens to withdraw a small favour.

In the comments the "thorn" is picked up as a hedgehog or a sea urchin; the "hard shell" is picked up as a turtle. The joke is not random insult: something the other side has just said immediately becomes a name that can be used again and again. [R15](../../sources/R15.md), [R17](../../sources/R17.md)

## Characters and how the lines are picked up

[Thanos](../../characters/灭霸.md) has to genuinely want to express emotion for the contrast when an acquaintance takes it apart to work. [Iron Man](../../characters/钢铁侠.md) and [Rocket Raccoon](../../characters/小浣熊.md) can first exchange their understanding, then one coins the nickname and the other puts the nickname into a real situation. Not everyone should just repeat the same sentence.

One reply in R15 directly assigns lines to Iron Man, Rocket and Thanos and ends with the supply of spicy strips being cut off; this is a version written by viewers in the comments, not dialogue verified in that video.

If "Now we're really in trouble" is used as a warning, first establish which partner notices the consequence and whom they are warning; for the existing object relationships see the [catchphrase entry](../../memes/这下玩大了.md), and do not mechanically assign it to Thanos or to any victim. This is a writing constraint and does not pretend to be a line that already appears in the R15 comments.

## Variants and boundaries

The comment section can complete a whole "posting - jeering - answering back" cycle on its own; it does not have to be cut into a campus scene the next day. Ability imagery can also take part in expressing sadness, for example a snap that changes the universe but cannot change one's mood; copy written by commenters must not be attributed to the original creator as dialogue.
